India Income Tax — Plain-Language Walkthrough

The same rule pack as examples/okf_sample_in.yaml, explained for people who don't write code. This is the second jurisdiction — it exists to prove OKF isn't US-shaped.

⚠️ Illustrative values. All tax values are illustrative — a real pack is verified against official sources (Income-tax Act, ITR utilities) by domain experts before release.

The pack in one sentence

Indian income tax for a salaried individual, FY 2025-26 — and it encodes the old-vs-new regime choice that every Indian filer faces.

Why this sample matters

The US pack proves OKF handles brackets and deductions. The India pack proves something harder: a regime choice. India doesn't have one tax schedule — it has two (old vs new), and the "right" answer depends on your deductions. In code-encoded tax apps this gets hard-coded into if/else spaghetti. In OKF it's just parameters keyed by regime plus rules — see §3.

2. The numbers the law sets

Standard deduction: ₹75,000 (new regime) vs ₹50,000 (old regime) — one parameter, two values, selected by regime.

3. The computation — in plain English

#RuleWhat it means
1Gross total income = salary + other income
2Standard deduction = the amount for your chosen regime
380C deductions = min(what you entered, ₹1,50,000 cap)
4Taxable income = income − standard deduction − (80C only if old regime)The engine multiplies capped 80C by a 0-or-1 regime flag — no special "if" needed
5Tax = progressive slabs for your regime
6Cess = 4% of tax
7Total = tax + cess

4. Where results land — ITR-1 (Sahaj)

Salary → Gross Total Income → Standard deduction → 80C deductions → Taxable Income → Tax → Cess → Total Payable.

That third test is the interesting one — it proves the regime boundary is enforced by the data, not by a special case in code.

The honest gap this sample exposes

Section 87A rebate is not modeled. Under the new regime, taxable income ≤ ₹12,00,000 gets a rebate up to ₹60,000 — which would zero out tax for many low-income filers. Expressing it needs a conditional the current OKF builtins don't have. The pack says so explicitly rather than hiding it — this is exactly the kind of edge case RFC question #1 (expression language) exists to resolve.

For Indian tax professionals: check the slabs, the cess, the 80C cap, and the regime mechanics against the actual Finance Act 2025 and ITR utility behavior. Where are we wrong? That's the contribution this project runs on.
