Problem statement

Filing your own taxes should be possible without surrendering your financial life to a cloud.

Every year, millions of people face the same choice: navigate dense government manuals alone, or hand their most sensitive financial data — income, deductions, identity numbers — to a corporation's servers. For many, professional guidance is simply out of reach.

Tax software isn't really selling you software. It's selling convenience at the price of your data: upsells at every step, dark patterns, and your financial profile monetized on someone else's infrastructure.

Meanwhile, the rules themselves — the actual tax law — are locked inside proprietary code or jargon-dense PDFs. You can't audit what the calculator did, because you can't see how it works.

TaxStack's bet: tax knowledge is a public good. The rules should be open data anyone can read and verify. The computation should happen on your machine. And the AI should translate the jargon — never touch the arithmetic.

How it will work

Three layers, each doing the one thing it's good at — in plain terms.

📐

The rulebook (OKF)

Think of tax law as a recipe book. OKF — the Open Knowledge Format — writes that recipe book as plain, versioned data anyone can read: the rates, the thresholds, the formulas, and worked examples that prove it works. A tax professional can check a rule the way they'd check a document — no programming needed.

⚙️

The calculator (engine)

A deterministic engine reads the rulebook and does the math — exactly, every time, with no guessing. Every number it produces can be traced back to a specific rule in a specific version of the rulebook. If the law changes, the rulebook gets a new version — nothing is ever silently edited.

🤖

The translator (local AI)

A small AI model runs on your computer and translates tax jargon into plain language — "why is my deduction limited?" — always citing its source. It reads the same open rulebook as the engine, but only for explanations and citations — never for math. If the AI is switched off, everything still works.

Why "local" matters

☁️ Cloud tax software

  • Your income, deductions, and ID numbers live on their servers
  • You're the product — data monetized, upsells built in
  • The calculation logic is a black box you can't audit
  • Subscription forever, even for a once-a-year task

🖥️ TaxStack

  • Zero financial data leaves your machine — by architecture, not policy
  • Every rule is open data, auditable by anyone
  • Every calculation traceable to a cited rule and version
  • Free and open source — forever

The iron rules

Three promises the project will not compromise — they're the whole point.

🚫🧮 The AI never does arithmetic

Every number comes from the deterministic engine, traceable to a specific rule in a specific version of the rulebook. The AI explains; the engine computes.

📎 Every AI claim carries a citation

Official publication or rule-pack entry. No citation, no claim. If the AI can't point to a source, it doesn't say it.

🔌 Fully functional with zero AI

The model is an enhancement, never a dependency. No internet, no model downloaded? The app still computes correctly — the AI just stays quiet.

Questions, answered

The ones people actually ask.

Is there an app I can download yet?

No — and we'd rather tell you that plainly. TaxStack is in vision stage (pre-RFC): we're publishing the vision, the architecture, and working rule packs first. Informal feedback is welcome now; a formal RFC may follow if the response justifies it. The roadmap is: feedback → OKF spec v0.1 → reference rule pack → engine → desktop app.

Will TaxStack be ready for this tax season?

Honestly — a full app this season is unlikely; we're in vision stage. But tax season is exactly why the timing matters: it's when the pain of gated tax knowledge is sharpest, and it's when the most people are looking for alternatives. Getting involved now — even just telling us what your filing looks like — is what shapes what ships first.

What is OKF, in one sentence?

The Open Knowledge Format — a published spec for encoding a jurisdiction's tax law as versioned, human-readable data (rates, thresholds, formulas, form mappings, worked tests) instead of hiding it inside code. Think of it as tax law written as an auditable recipe book. Details in the manifesto →

Will the AI do my taxes?

No — that's the iron rule. The AI runs locally and explains: it translates jargon, walks you through questions, and cites its sources. Every number comes from the deterministic engine. If the AI hallucinates, the worst case is a confusing sentence — never a wrong tax calculation. And with the AI off, the app still works.

Is my data really safe?

TaxStack's core guarantee: zero financial data leaves your machine. No cloud account, no telemetry, no external API calls for tax processing or AI reasoning. AI runs on a local model via an on-device runtime (under evaluation — Ollama, llama.cpp, or equivalent), with encrypted local storage. We published a threat model listing exactly what we protect and how — and we invite scrutiny of it.

Do I need to know how to code to help?

No. If you can read a tax manual, you can contribute — reviewing rule packs, checking worked examples against official publications, and telling us where our mental model of your work is wrong. Engineers are equally welcome; the engine needs no tax knowledge at all. Start here →

Which countries/jurisdictions will be supported?

The design is jurisdiction-agnostic — every country's rules are a versioned "pack" authored by that country's own community. The two sample packs (US federal, India) prove the format works across very different systems. Which jurisdiction becomes the reference implementation is an open design question — we want your nomination and reasoning.

Is this a replacement for tax professionals?

No — explicitly not. TaxStack is an educational and assistive tool. For complex situations it helps you arrive informed and prepared, and it points you toward professional help when warranted. Tax professionals aren't the competition — they're the authors and reviewers of the very rules the engine runs on.

Why should I trust a pre-code project?

Fair question. We publish everything before building: the manifesto with its honest prior-art analysis, the architecture's known risks, a threat model, and working sample packs with test cases you can verify yourself. Trust here isn't claimed — it's designed in: golden tests, versioned packs, citations, and a correction process where fixes ship as new versions, never silent edits.

TaxStack is an educational and assistive tool — not tax or financial advice, and not a licensed tax preparer.
