US Federal Income Tax — Plain-Language Walkthrough

The same rule pack as examples/okf_sample_us.yaml, explained for people who don't write code. YAML is the machine encoding; this page is the expert review surface. A second pack — India — proves the format isn't US-shaped.

In the full system, this walkthrough will be generated automatically from the pack — never hand-maintained — so what a tax professional reviews is always exactly what the engine computes. A friendly authoring/editing UI is an open RFC question, not a v0 promise.

⚠️ Illustrative values. All tax values in the sample are illustrative — a real pack is verified against official sources by domain experts before release.

The pack in one sentence

US federal income tax, tax year 2025, for a single filer — the simplest slice that proves the whole pipeline works.

Every rule pack answers four questions: What do we ask the taxpayer? What numbers does the law set? How do we compute? Where do results land on the official form?

3. The computation ("rules") — in plain English

The engine evaluates these in order; each one names its legal source and carries the explanation the AI cites:

#RuleWhat it means
1AGI = gross income − adjustmentsIncome after above-the-line deductions
2Deduction used = larger of (itemized deductions, standard deduction for your status)You get whichever is bigger
3Taxable income = max(0, AGI − deduction used)Never below zero
4Tax before credits = progressive tax on taxable incomeEach slice taxed at its bracket's rate — higher rates apply only to income above each threshold
5Total tax = max(0, tax before credits − credits)Credits reduce the bill dollar-for-dollar

Key property: every rule traces to an official publication, uses only a restricted set of operations (no arbitrary code), and every number the app shows you can be traced back to a specific rule in a specific pack version.

What this walkthrough proves — and doesn't

  • ✅ Proves: the shape of OKF is complete enough to express real tax law — inputs, parameters, rules, form mappings, and verifiable tests in one auditable file.
  • ⚠️ Doesn't prove: that a tax professional can author this YAML unaided. That's an open RFC question — the long-term goal is a friendly editing surface over this same canonical format. For now, domain experts review the rendered pack (this page) and flag corrections; engineers handle the YAML encoding.

Try it yourself: if you file US federal taxes, check the rules and test cases above against the official 2025 instructions. Where are we wrong? That's exactly the feedback this project exists to get.
